## The Craft of Writing Effectively
Learning goal: Improve writing, and also better discern which research problems are worth pursuing.

At Chicago, they came to the realization that academics (faculty) need more help than freshmen.
Why? They are experts. They are writing about a topic with which they have expert knowledge, about something in the world.
So we think about the world. You use writing to help yourself do your thinking, about this difficult topic.
When you write your material, you think as your write, and you create a text, for the readers to change the way they see the world.
People read your work because it is valuable.
Valuable is subjective: value lies in what a specific set of readers deems valuable. (Textbooks are valuable to undergrads, etc.)
When people say that they don't think your writing is valuable, you will assume they don't understand it and try to explain it, which is the wrong move.
In Western Academia, nothing is accepted as knowledge until it has been challenged by someone competent enough to challenge it.
You only explain once you have generated value (showing that it is valuable) and persuasion having begun. Don't explain first.
Every community of readers (who deem certain information to be valuable and others not) have a "code" of words they use to communicate that value.
You can tell people they are wrong, but do so under the code.
Goal of writing: change other people's thoughts. To change the discussion within a field.

Now, we are going to be looking at the code.
- Anomaly, inconsistent, but, however, although
	- These words create instability.

Start with a problem, and give a solution.
- To introduce the problem, use an instability. Show that this instability creates cost, and solving it creates benefit. We don't care about inconsistencies that don't cost us anything.
- Good literature reviews emphasizes the problem. It introduces the communities that you are dealing with.

Takeaway
- It's really important to think about the communities that exist within a community, and what problems they deal with.
- Write to address the things that they care about.
- To succeed in my masters, I am studying ideas in the way that they are expressed and formed, and the instabilities in systems of thought that need to be resolved.
- Start being sensitive to this when you read papers, by looking at what problems they think are interesting, and the problems that underlie what is being done.
## Mathematical Methods and Human Thought in the Age of AI
Learning Goals
- Terence Tao's perspective, as a mathematician, on how we will integrate with AI
	- This will help me understand my role as a researcher using AI (not getting replaced by AI)
	- How our thoughts, ideas, and the development of new ideas is shaped in good and bad ways by AI.
	- Hopefully will tell me something too about the future of AI + Humanity in general.

The two authors of this paper study completely different fields (math and art) but both use AI; they are seeking to communicate insight into why and how these tools should be used, and ways in which their development will be more rather than less beneficial.
They contemplate ethical issues and risks, as well as benefits and outcomes.
The genie is out of the bottle... Faustian Bargain.

Past revolutions, though changing which jobs people did, did not challenge answers to philosophical questions about the nature of work. AI does, because we now have systems which can mimic human work perfectly, without sharing the same values/thought processes, and challenge what intelligence, understanding, and creativity are.

### Mathematics

In math, AI generates "good looking" outputs with no understanding (which can be seen as it makes nonsensical mistakes occasionally, even now) and is being developed by trying to reduce the number of non-sensical outputs.

In theory, mathematics is built on mathematics, and built up using logical rules of inference to construct the entire system, each step can be checked. In practice, however, mathematicians gloss over plausible arguments, and only scrutinize results they doubt. Mathematicians have an intuition about how convincing an argument is. As proofs get longer, that "smell" is used where checking a proof line by line would be too tedious. Formalization, and formal verification might be able to help, though it can be tedious.

Not all of the field is formal... intuitions, discussions on how proof techniques might relate to other problems and fields, also make up lots of valuable mathematical research. Also, the "smell" of a proof is lost if people use chat to polish the writing.

Computers were already being used... for example, with the proof of the four colour problem. But with new AI models, deductive logical proofs and calculations can be done automatically, and the softer aspects of math reasoning (motivation, trial and error, experimental evidence) that lead to the working argument.

If we rely too much on AI we can publish many papers with new, correct results that don't contribute to the interesting narratives in the research, and don't build any helpful intuition. This is similar to the ideas in "The Craft of Writing Effectively"... we want to assert the value of our ideas, not just come up with original ideas.

Intellectual property/credit assignment is a problem. Circular citation loops: AI creates a source (originally intended as a survey paper or reference hub) that then itself gets cited, which gives us a loop.

### Societal Costs and Benefits
- Use cases should be assessed based on beneficiaries
	- Large economic gain for a small number of individuals at the cost of the majority is unacceptable
	- Hard to measure... resources used vs resources gained? monetary gains and losses? quality of life and happiness?
	- Right now: optimizing for power and broad impact, implementing in every sphere of society without thinking about benefit
- The costs of AI
	- AI can only be built by massive companies with an enormous amount of capital which then incentivizes profit.
	- Environmental costs are extreme.
	- Information cost... AI isn't grounded in truth or intuition as humans are... AI can contaminate this information base and lead to its own destruction.
- Digital Divide
	- Could be a divide between those willing to have lower ethical standards (and so can take better advantage of AI) and those who don't.
	- Some models (that only certain people have access to) may be significantly more powerful than others.
	- Smaller models that do what just what you need and no more might be the best solution.
	- Public government-funded AI models could democratize use.
- Harm Reduction
	- Wikipedia is a good example: though problematic use at first, guidelines were set up over time so that it was acceptable and seen as a good thing.
	- We need to agree on values before we can make meaningful progress towards responsible use.
### The Human/AI Interface
- Short term usage
	- We use it every once in a while in small ways which we find helpful... questions, summaries, etc. If we use it too much it can degrade our work/reduce efficiency.
- Medium term usage
	- Use it for verification, validation, testing, maintenance
- Long term
	- Hairy questions asked about the value of human intellect... is something more valuable precisely because humans made it? Or are there aspects of human intelligence that AI will never be able to replicate?
	- Will we have a society where humans no longer have to do any intellectual work at all?
	- The above two views are straw-man extremes... in reality, we continue to play chess even though chess engines can beat the best grandmasters.
- Copernican view
	- In the past, we believed the earth was the center of the universe, but now we see we are one planet of many. That was threatening at first, but now we just recognize that although many other planets exist, our planet is the most important to us.
	- In the same way, we don't have to argue which intellectual sphere is the "center" for us to have our interests and attachments on the human intellectual sphere.
### Conclusion
- Currently implementation and integration of AI is pretty chaotic.
- We will likely need some external force or regulation to implement values that lead to these tools benefiting all humanity rather than a small minority at the cost of the rest.
- AI use in mathematics shows us potential benefits of its use but also problems with its use in research.
	- Valuable qualities for each: humans have a level of understanding that AI does not, that is grounded in intuition about the real world, whereas AI just trains how to make good looking outputs. Humans supply intuition, framing, meaning, and direction while AI does deduction, computation, and exhaustive search.
